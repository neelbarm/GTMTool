    How it scores

    No model. A ruler.

    Sameness index
    The share of your copy covered by phrases drawn from a lexicon of interchangeable B2B language, weighted by how empty each phrase is, minus credit for specifics: numbers, named buyers, named alternatives, and concrete nouns. 0 means every phrase is doing work only you could claim. 100 means a competitor could paste it in without edits.
    The five parts
    Who it is for, what it replaces, why it is different, what the buyer gets, and the proof. Each is detected by pattern, so a sentence that names a buyer, an alternative, or a number passes. The method follows the positioning framework that April Dunford describes in Obviously Awesome.
    The numbers in the header
    From Wynter's June 2026 differentiation survey of 100 B2B SaaS marketing leaders (64% find vendors hard to tell apart from their websites; they matched unbranded copy to brand at 1.86 of 5) and Bain's 2026 B2B Growth Agenda survey of 1,000+ B2B leaders (4% have a strong value proposition). Both are vendor-run studies with modest samples. The direction is consistent with TrustRadius's 2022 finding that unclear messaging is a top-three reason buyers walk.
    Open code, open data
    The scoring model, the server, and the page are MIT licensed. The index is downloadable as CSV from the footer. If you quote a number from it, link back so the next person can check it.
    What it cannot do
    It cannot tell whether a specific claim is true, and it will reward a specific lie. Treat it as a linter, not a judge. Scoring runs in your browser. Nothing leaves the page unless you add it to the index.
